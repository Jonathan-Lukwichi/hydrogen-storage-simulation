Why this exists

Three months of model-building stands between a postgrad and a defensible simulation.

Coupled multi-physics simulations of hydrogen behaviour in HEAs are no longer scientifically optional — and they're not actually hard to set up correctly. They're just slow, expensive and politically tedious to validate. HydroHEA exists to remove those three frictions.

12 wk

COMSOL setup takes weeks

Defining a 2-D plate, calibrating Arrhenius D(T), coupling Transport of Diluted Species to Heat Transfer and Solid Mechanics, and running parameter sweeps consumes more time than most thesis chapters allow.

R 200k+

The licence is unreachable

A COMSOL Multiphysics seat with the modules required for coupled transport + heat + structural mechanics costs upwards of R 200 000 per year. For most South African universities, HySA centres, and small consultancies, that's a non-starter.

< 5 %

Reviewers ask for validation

Simulation outputs that haven't been mesh-validated (≤ 5 % deviation across coarse/medium/fine) and cross-referenced against published experimental data weaken the credibility of any subsequent publication.

What HydroHEA delivers

Four capabilities — no more, no less.

We don't predict optimal compositions. We don't run DFT. We don't replace experiments. We deliver four well-scoped, scientifically defensible services that materials groups actually need.

⬡

1 · Multi-physics simulator

Submit your alloy composition, geometry and boundary conditions. We run a validated coupled COMSOL-based simulation of hydrogen transport, thermal field and stress evolution over your specified time window.

  • Transport of Diluted Species — Fickian diffusion with Arrhenius D(T) = D₀ · exp(−Q/RT)
  • Heat Transfer in Solids — bulk thermal evolution coupled to boundary conditions
  • Solid Mechanics — linear-elastic von Mises stress driven by hydride lattice expansion
  • Output: time-dependent c(x, t), T(x, t) and σ_vM(x, t) fields, exported as CSV + figures
◐

2 · Mesh-validated results

Every simulation is run at three mesh densities — coarse, medium and fine — and reported only when deviations remain under 5 %. The mesh-independence trace ships with every deliverable.

  • Coarse · medium · fine triangular meshes per study
  • Per-variable deviation against the fine-mesh reference
  • Acceptance threshold: ≤ 5 % on concentration, temperature and stress
  • Audit-ready convergence report attached to every run
◊

3 · Literature cross-referencing

Outputs are automatically benchmarked against published experimental data for comparable HEA systems — AlCoFeNi, AlCrFeNi, AlFeNiTi, AlFeNiCu — to establish physical plausibility before you submit a paper for review.

  • Diffusion-coefficient comparison against published Arrhenius fits
  • Hydride-formation enthalpy bounds against measured ranges
  • Cross-system stress and uptake plausibility checks
  • Cited references inside every report
◈

4 · Materials reference library

A curated, searchable database of thermophysical properties, diffusion parameters and elastic constants for Al–Fe–Ni-based and related HEA systems, drawn from peer-reviewed sources. Weeks of literature mining, indexed.

  • Density, Young's modulus, Poisson ratio, partial molar volume of H
  • Arrhenius pre-exponentials and activation energies by phase (BCC, FCC, C15)
  • Hydride formation enthalpies with literature provenance
  • One-click export of parameters into your own COMSOL or scikit-fem model
Methodology

Built on peer-reviewed research at TUT.

HydroHEA's methodology is the operationalised form of a research programme conducted at Tshwane University of Technology's Department of Chemical, Metallurgical and Materials Engineering by Christian Emmanuel Lukwichi, under the supervision of Prof. Patricia Popoola and Dr. L.R. Kanyane.

  • 01

    Coupled multi-physics on a 2-D slab

    A 1 mm × 5 mm cross-section is meshed with linear triangular elements. The same node set carries Transport of Diluted Species, Heat Transfer in Solids and Solid Mechanics so concentration, temperature and stress are solved consistently at every time step.

  • 02

    Arrhenius-temperature-dependent diffusion

    D(T) = D₀ · exp(−Q / RT) with D₀ and Q calibrated from the published HEA literature for the system you submit. The diffusion coefficient varies with the temperature field at every node, not a global constant.

  • 03

    Mesh-independence as a release criterion

    Every study runs at three mesh densities. We only ship results when concentration, temperature and stress all converge under 5 % deviation against the fine-mesh reference — the engineering convention for trustworthy FEM.

  • 04

    Literature cross-referencing as a final gate

    Before delivery, outputs are checked against the experimental ranges reported for AlCoFeNi, AlCrFeNi, AlFeNiTi and AlFeNiCu. Anomalous outputs trigger a re-run, not a quiet caveat.

Governing equations ∂c/∂t = ∇·(D(T)∇c) D(T) = D₀ · exp(−Q / RT) ρ Cₚ ∂T/∂t = ∇·(k ∇T) + Q_H σ_vM = E · β · c / (1 − 2ν)

The constitutive law is linear elasticity coupled to hydrogen-induced lattice dilation; the diffusion coefficient is temperature-dependent through Arrhenius; the thermal field exchanges energy with hydride formation through a source term.

Honest answers

Frequently asked questions.

  • Can HydroHEA predict the best alloy composition for hydrogen storage?
    No. We simulate the behaviour of the compositions you specify. Composition design — choosing which elements to mix and in what fractions — remains the researcher's domain. We're a simulator, not an alloy-discovery oracle.
  • Does HydroHEA replace experimental validation?
    No. Simulations guide and reduce experiments; they do not replace them. Our cross-referencing against published experimental data is a plausibility check, not a substitute for your own measurements. We expect every customer to validate the simulation against their own samples before publication.
  • What alloy systems are supported today?
    Al–Fe–Ni-based HEAs in BCC and FCC phases, plus the closely related transition-metal systems (AlCoFeNi, AlCrFeNi, AlFeNiTi, AlFeNiCu). On the roadmap: Mg-based hydrides (Mg₂Ni, MgH₂ derivatives), Ti–V–Nb–Cr systems and Zr-bearing HEAs. Request other systems via the pilot form — we may already be modelling them internally.
  • Do I need a COMSOL licence?
    No. We run every simulation on our infrastructure and return CSVs, figures and a PDF report. You don't need COMSOL, scikit-fem, FEniCS, or any FEM toolchain installed locally.
  • How do you ensure results are trustworthy?
    Two gates. (1) Mesh-independence: every study runs at three mesh densities and we only release results when all monitored quantities converge below 5 % deviation against the fine-mesh reference. (2) Literature cross-referencing: outputs must fall inside the experimentally reported ranges for comparable HEA systems before delivery. Anomalies trigger an internal review, not a footnote in your report.
  • What does a delivery actually contain?
    A PDF engineering report (cover, executive summary, full inputs, time-resolved outputs, mesh-sensitivity trace, literature cross-reference, methodology), the underlying time-series data as CSV, and the field figures (concentration / temperature / stress maps) as both PNG and SVG. Everything is reproducible from the input file we hold for your run.
  • Who owns the simulation outputs?
    You do. HydroHEA retains the right to reuse the anonymised methodology trace for internal QA and aggregate publication statistics. Your composition, geometry and results are yours to publish, patent or commercialise as you see fit. Enterprise contracts include explicit IP-protection terms.
